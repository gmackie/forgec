# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Argue about the work, not the person doing it. Assume the
other person is trying to build something good and is working with less context
than you have.

## What is expected

- Give feedback on code, designs and claims — directly, specifically, and with
  a reason. "This is wrong because X" is welcome. "This is bad" is not useful,
  and "you are bad" is not acceptable.
- Accept that other people have constraints you cannot see, and ask about them
  before deciding they are wrong.
- When you are shown to be wrong, say so and move on. That is a normal part of
  engineering, not a loss.
- Respect a maintainer's "no". Not every good idea belongs in this project, and
  an unanswered issue is a capacity problem, not a slight.

## What is not acceptable

Harassment in any form; demeaning, insulting or derogatory comments; personal
or political attacks; publishing other people's private information; sustained
disruption of discussions; unwelcome sexual attention; and advocating for or
encouraging any of the above.

This applies to issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages, code
comments, and any other space where you are representing this project.

## Reporting

Report conduct problems privately to the repository owner at
**conduct@forgegraf.com**, or through GitHub's own
[reporting tools](https://docs.github.com/communities/maintaining-your-safety-on-github/reporting-abuse-or-spam)
if the problem involves the maintainers.

Reports are read by the maintainers only. You will get an acknowledgement
within 3 business days. We will tell you what we decided; we will not share
your report or your identity with the person you reported beyond what is
necessary to act on it.

## Consequences

Depending on severity and history: a private word, a public correction, edited
or removed comments, a temporary ban from interaction, or a permanent ban.
Maintainers who do not uphold this document in good faith are subject to the
same consequences as anyone else.
